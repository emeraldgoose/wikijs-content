# Decision-making strategies for model retraining and maintenance

> **Level**: Intermediate  
> **Source**: [How we knew COVID was over (and what our models had to unlearn)](#)  
> **Last Updated**: 2026-08-28

## Introduction

Decision-making strategies for model retraining and maintenance encompass the protocols used to determine when machine learning systems require updates, reconstruction, or retention based on performance drift and error analysis. This distinction is vital because forecast errors in production often compound as downstream teams build operational plans upon the data, where small biases can evolve into significant business risks. Key use cases include diagnosing persistent directional bias that survives routine refreshes, managing model stability during structural breaks such as global disruptions, and evaluating whether incremental data updates suffice compared to full model rebuilding. Ultimately, these strategies prioritize understanding the source of variance over automatic retraining to ensure long-term reliability in high-stakes environments.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: Forecasts as High-Stakes Risk Managerment
The text emphasizes that model maintenance is driven by business risk rather than purely technical accuracy because forecasts serve as foundational inputs for the rest of the organization.
*   **Downstream Dependencies:** Many other teams build their planning and decisions directly on top of these forecasts (e.g., demand, bookings, cancellations).
*   **Compounding Bias:** A small bias that might be considered a "clean miss" in an academic setting becomes a significant risk in production when multiplied across thousands of markets and decisions.
*   **Risk Over Methods:** When a forecast drifts, the decision to intervene is fundamentally a risk question. The cost of a "casually wrong" forecast outweighs the cost of intervention, requiring a disciplined approach to maintenance.

### Concept 2: Distinguishing the "Three Decisions" of Model Updates
A common mistake is treating "retraining" as a single, monolithic action. The core strategy involves recognizing three distinct maintenance paths that require different levels of effort and urgency.
*   **Refitting:** The "cheaper option" involving updating the model with recent data while keeping the same model structure and features.
*   **Respecifying (Rebuilding):** Implied as a distinct action from refitting, this involves changing the model's structure or logic to address fundamental shifts.
*   **Holding:** The strategic decision to leave a model alone despite observed issues, acknowledging that not all drift requires a technical intervention.

### Concept 3: Diagnostic Discipline Over Reactive Refreshing
Instead of automatically assuming a routine data refresh will fix issues, the strategy requires investigating the root cause of model drift before taking action.
*   **Persisting Bias:** If a forecast misses in the same direction over multiple quarters even after routine refreshes, the issue is likely not solved by simply pulling in more data.
*   **Source Analysis:** Teams must understand the *source* of the bias (e.g., structural change in the environment vs. temporary noise) rather than hoping an update will eliminate it.
*   **Avoiding Automation:** Deciding to retrain is not just a technical trigger; it is a strategic choice that should not be made blindly if the underlying drivers of bias are not understood.
