# Adaptive Time Series Forecasting During Unprecedented Shocks

## Introduction

Adaptive Time Series Forecasting During Unprecedented Shocks is a statistical methodology designed to maintain predictive accuracy when historical data patterns become invalid due to sudden, systemic disruptions. This approach mitigates the risk of standard models failing confidently during non-stationary regimes, where past behavior no longer indicates future performance. By leveraging auxiliary signals, such as sequential geographic recovery data or cross-sectional correlations, these systems adapt to asynchronous changes without relying solely on broken temporal trends. This resilience is critical for urban planning, logistics, and financial stability during crises. Key use cases include forecasting demand recovery during pandemics, optimizing supply chains amidst global disruptions, and modeling financial market volatility. Consequently, organizations can generate reliable projections even when local historical data is scarce or irrelevant.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: The Breakdown of Historical Continuity Assumptions
*   **Implicit Dependency:** Almost every standard forecasting system assumes the future will resemble the past, training on historical data to predict future performance.
*   **Confident Failure:** When a novel shock occurs (like the acute phase of COVID-19), this assumption breaks. Models do not gracefully degrade but instead fail confidently, producing precise, well-calibrated intervals around the wrong answers.
*   **Data Scarcity Latency:** The traditional solution of waiting for each affected market to accumulate its own post-shock data before retraining results in months of blind forecasting when timely projections are most needed.

### Concept 2: Asynchronous and Overlapping Recovery Regimes
*   **Lack of Coherence:** Post-shock periods (such as late 2020 through 2022) are often not single, coherent regimes but sequences of overlapping changes.
*   **Factors of Asynchronicity:** Recovery timelines are staggered due to variables like vaccine rollouts, border reopenings, and new variants, each following different country-level or corridor-specific schedules.
*   **Uneven Rebound:** Demand does not recover uniformly; instead, it rebounds unevenly across the world with no historical precedent or single governing pattern to guide a localized model.

### Concept 3: Geospatial Signal Borrowing and Propagation
*   **Shifting the Domain:** When historical data is insufficient ("history fails"), the solution shifts from temporal reliance to spatial leverage ("borrow from geography").
*   **Sequential Signals:** The system utilizes recovery signals from geographic markets that opened up earlier to inform forecasts for markets that are recovering later.
*   **Prior Propagation:** By propagating these prior signals, the model can generate reliable corridor-level forecasts even when the local data for a specific destination remains scarce or non-existent.
