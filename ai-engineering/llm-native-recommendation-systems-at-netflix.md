# LLM-Native Recommendation Systems at Netflix

> **Level**: Advanced  
> **Source**: [GenRec: Towards LLM-Native Recommendation at Netflix](#)  
> **Last Updated**: 2026-08-28

## Introduction

LLM-native recommendation systems at Netflix denote architectures utilizing large language models, specifically the GenRec framework, to rank content based on textual representations of user history and item metadata rather than traditional hand-crafted feature engineering. This approach addresses the complexity of existing specialized models, which require significant architectural changes to accommodate new content types or product surfaces. By leveraging semantic understanding, the system facilitates integrating diverse media formats, including movies, series, games, and podcasts, within a unified representation space. Key applications focus on post-training internal foundation models to mitigate production challenges such as hallucination, popularity bias, and ignored business constraints. Ultimately, this initiative aims to simplify feature engineering while enhancing personalization across the Netflix ecosystem.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: Limitations of Traditional Recommendation Stacks
Netflix's current production models rely on a complex infrastructure that creates barriers for scaling and innovation.
*   **Heavy Reliance on Features:** The system depends on thousands of hand-crafted features regarding users, items, and interactions.
*   **Specialized Architectures:** Model structures are specifically designed for sequence modeling, feature interactions, and multi-task objectives.
*   **High Onboarding Cost:** Adding new content types (e.g., games, live) or product surfaces requires significant engineering work, including feature engineering, architecture changes, and infrastructure updates.

### Concept 2: Challenges of Off-the-Shelf LLMs
While Large Language Models (LLMs) offer new possibilities, generic models are not immediately suitable for production recommendation tasks.
*   **Accuracy Issues:** Standard LLMs tend to over-recommend globally popular content rather than personalized items.
*   **Hallucination Risks:** They often generate or recommend items that are not actually available in the catalog.
*   **Constraint Violations:** They frequently ignore specific business constraints required for a commercial platform.
*   **Personalization Gaps:** Off-the-shelf models provide limited ability to adapt to individual user histories without specific tuning.

### Concept 3: GenRec Architecture and Post-Training
GenRec is designed as an LLM-backed ranker that bridges the gap between generic LLM capabilities and Netflix-specific requirements.
*   **Internal Foundation Model:** Instead of using public models, GenRec utilizes an internal foundation LLM.
*   **Domain-Specific Post-Training:** The model is post-trained on Netflix-specific data and objectives to align with the platform's ecosystem.
*   **Semantic Representation:** User histories and item metadata are represented directly as natural language within a shared semantic space.
*   **Prompt-Driven Steering:** Recommendations are steered via natural-language prompts rather than raw numerical embeddings alone.

### Concept 4: Inference Pipeline and Efficiency
The GenRec pipeline transforms raw data into rankings through a streamlined process optimized for performance and data efficiency.
*   **Context Engineering:** Raw logs of user history, item metadata, and context are transformed into natural-language prompts.
*   **Scoring Mechanism:** The system runs on vLLM in "prefill-only" mode to output scores for each catalog item efficiently.
*   **Data Efficiency:** The LLM-based approach can match or exceed mature production systems while relying on far fewer labeled examples and input signals.
*   **Result Generation:** The final output yields a recommendation ranking based on the calculated scores.
