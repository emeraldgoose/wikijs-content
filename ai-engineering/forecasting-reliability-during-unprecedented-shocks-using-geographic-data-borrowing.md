# Forecasting reliability during unprecedented shocks using geographic data borrowing

> **Level**: Intermediate  
> **Source**: [When history fails you, borrow from geography](#)  
> **Last Updated**: 2026-08-28

## Introduction

Geographic data borrowing is a forecasting methodology that leverages recovery signals from comparable or chronologically preceding regions to predict demand in markets lacking sufficient local historical data during unprecedented shocks. This approach addresses the critical failure point of traditional time-series models, which rely on the assumption that future conditions resemble past patterns, thereby preventing confident yet inaccurate projections during systemic disruptions. By utilizing sequential geographic recovery signals and prior propagation, analysts can generate reliable corridor-level forecasts even when demand rebounds unevenly across different locales. Key use cases include pandemic recovery modeling, such as tracking asynchronous vaccine rollouts and border reopenings, as well as forecasting performance during supply chain interruptions or varying regional regulatory changes.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: Inherent Failure of Historical Assumptions
*   **Stationarity Breakdown:** Conventional forecasting systems operate on the implicit assumption that the future will resemble the past, relying on historical data for training and validation.
*   **Confident Failure:** When unprecedented shocks occur, this assumption breaks, causing models to "fail confidently" rather than degrading gracefully.
*   **Precision without Accuracy:** In shoked environments, models produce precise, well-calibrated intervals around the wrong answers because they are extrapolating patterns that no longer exist.

### Concept 2: Asynchronous Recovery Dynamics
*   **Non-Uniform Regimes:** Post-shock periods are not single, coherent regimes; they consist of a sequence of overlapping, asynchronous changes.
*   **Staggered Timelines:** Recovery drivers such as vaccine rollouts, border reopenings, and new variant reclosures follow different timelines for every country and travel corridor.
*   **Lack of Precedent:** Demand rebounds unevenly across the globe in ways that have no historical precedent and lack a single governing pattern.

### Concept 3: The Blind Spot of Local-Only Forecasting
*   **Standard Reliance:** The typical response to a shock is to wait for each affected market to accumulate its own post-shock data before retraining locally.
*   **Forecasting Blindness:** Waiting for markets to settle forces forecasters to look blind for months, precisely when timely projections are most needed.
*   **Data Scarcity:** The critical need for timing projections coincides with the moment local data is too scarce to support reliable models.

### Concept 4: Geographic Data Borrowing Framework
*   **Spatial Substitution:** Instead of waiting for local history, the model borrows data from geographies that recovered earlier to inform those recovering later.
*   **Sequential Signals:** It utilizes sequential geographic recovery signals to detect patterns of propagation across different regions.
*   **Prior Propagation:** By applying prior propagation, the system generates reliable corridor-level forecasts even when local historical data is unavailable or insufficient.
