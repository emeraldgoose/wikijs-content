# Forecasting strategies for unprecedented market shocks using geographic data borrowing

## Introduction

Forecasting strategies for unprecedented market shocks using geographic data borrowing are analytical methods leveraging recovery signals from analogous geographic markets to predict demand in regions with scarce data during systemic disruptions. This approach addresses the limitation of traditional time-series models, which fail when historical patterns do not inform future performance, by applying spatial propagation instead of relying on local history. Primary use cases involve modeling asynchronous recoveries, such as uneven pandemic reopenings, allowing organizations to generate reliable corridor-level forecasts when local historical patterns are absent or misleading.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: The Failure of Temporal Extrapolation in Shocks
Standard forecasting models typically rely on the implicit assumption that future performance will resemble historical patterns. During unprecedented shocks, this assumption fails, causing models to produce precise but incorrect predictions.
*   **Historical Reliance:** Most systems are trained on historical data and validated on holdout periods, expecting past patterns to indicate future performance.
*   **Confident Failure:** When assumptions break during a shock (e.g., COVID-19), models do not degrade gracefully; instead, they generate well-calibrated intervals around the wrong answer.
*   **Data Latency:** Waiting for affected markets to accumulate their own post-shock historical data results in forecasting blindly for months when projections are most needed.

### Concept 2: Asynchronous and Uneven Recovery Regimes
Post-shock recovery is rarely a single, coherent timeline. Instead, markets transition through overlapping regimes driven by external factors that vary geographically.
*   **Non-Uniform Recovery:** Demand does not rebound uniformly; different regions recover at different speeds and patterns with no historical precedent.
*   **Staggered Timelines:** Specific drivers such as vaccine rollouts, border reopenings, and variant reclosures affect different traveler origin-destination corridors at different moments.
*   **Lack of Governing Pattern:** The recovery period often consists of asynchronous changes rather than a unified trend, making local single-market analysis insufficient.

### Concept 3: Sequential Geographic Data Borrowing
To overcome the lack of local historical data, the strategy involves "borrowing" information from markets that recovered earlier, using geography as a proxy for time.
*   **Geographic Signals:** Instead of waiting for local data to build up, forecasters track sequential recovery signals from other markets that are ahead in the recovery cycle.
*   **Prior Propagation:** Information regarding recovery trends is propagated from markets with prior data to those with scarce data, establishing reliable corridor-level forecasts.
*   **Bypassing Local Scarcity:** This method allows for reliable projections during the volatile post-shock phase without requiring each specific market to settle or accumulate its own timeline of events first.
