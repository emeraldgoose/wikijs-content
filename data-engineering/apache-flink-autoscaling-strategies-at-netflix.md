# Apache Flink Autoscaling Strategies at Netflix

> **Level**: Advanced  
> **Source**: [A Tale of Two Flink Autoscalers](#)  
> **Last Updated**: 2026-08-28

## Introduction

Apache Flink autoscaling strategies at Netflix refer to the dynamic resource management methodologies employed to optimize performance across the company's extensive stream processing infrastructure. Vital for managing over 30,000 jobs across multiple AWS regions, these strategies prevent resource wastage and latency by adapting to fluctuating workloads like daily cycles and event spikes. Netflix currently utilizes both a legacy internal autoscaler and the Apache Flink community version while converging on the open-source solution. Primary applications include personalization, advertising, live event processing, and stateful pipelines distributed via the Data Mesh platform. This infrastructural evolution highlights critical industry challenges regarding metric selection, cost optimization, and the operational overhead of maintaining proprietary versus community-driven systems.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: Operational Scale and Job Heterogeneity
The need for autonomous autoscaling at Netflix is driven by an ecosystem that is too large for manual intervention and too complex for uniform handling.
*   **Massive Volume:** Netflix operates over 30,000 Flink jobs across multiple AWS regions, a scale where manual provisioning is impossible.
*   **Platform Automation:** The majority of jobs are not deployed by humans but are automatically generated by the internal **Data Mesh** platform.
*   **Workload Diversity:** Jobs vary significantly, ranging from simple data shuffling between Kafka topics to complex, stateful pipelines involving joins, branches, and terabytes of state.
*   **Variable Load:** Traffic is not static; it fluctuates based on daily cycles, content launches, and regional failovers, necessitating dynamic resource adjustment.

### Concept 2: The High Cost of Scaling Operations
Scaling is treated not as a free operation, but as a potentially disruptive event that creates latency and consumes time, creating a difficult trade-off between efficiency and stability.
*   **Scaling Penalties:** In this architecture, a scaling action typically requires taking a **savepoint**, gracefully stopping the job, and restarting it at a new size.
*   **Latency Risks:** For large stateful jobs, this process can take minutes, which poses a risk of application lag during surges.
*   **Provisioning Trade-off:** Over-provisioning for peak load is wasteful of resources, while under-provisioning causes lag; autoscaling aims to find the middle ground without human intervention.
*   **Stateful Complexity:** Stateful operations add weight to the scaling process, making the "stop and restart" cycle more expensive than in stateless environments.

### Concept 3: Converging from In-House to Open Source
Netflix employed a strategy of running two different autoscalers simultaneously to transition from a custom solution to a community-supported one, prioritizing long-term maintainability over immediate control.
*   **Legacy System:** The first autoscaler was an in-house solution built around 2019, designed to handle specific initial workloads.
*   **Community System:** The second autoscaler is the open-source Apache Flink autoscaler, capable of handling workloads the custom system could not.
*   **Parallel Operation:** Both systems currently run in production, allowing Netflix to test convergence without risking service stability.
*   **Strategic Deprecation:** The goal is to steadily converge entirely on the open-source solution to reduce the burden of maintaining custom infrastructure.

### Concept 4: Infrastructure Economics and Metrics
A core lesson learned during the transition involves the true cost of ownership regarding metrics and the maintenance of custom infrastructure.
*   **Metric Discipline:** There are "hard lessons" learned specifically regarding the quality and utility of metrics used to drive scaling decisions.
*   **Cost of Customization:** Maintaining homegrown infrastructure carries a "real price" that often outweighs the benefits of customization when mature open-source alternatives exist.
*   **Maintenance Overhead:** The philosophy emphasizes avoiding the creation of infrastructure systems ("two autoscalers") that require more management than the workload itself ("one more than we want").
*   **Scalability of Management:** The chosen strategy must scale not just with the jobs (30,000+), but with the engineering effort required to manage the autoscaler itself.
