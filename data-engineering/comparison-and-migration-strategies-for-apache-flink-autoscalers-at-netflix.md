# Comparison and migration strategies for Apache Flink autoscalers at Netflix

> **Level**: Advanced  
> **Source**: [A Tale of Two Flink Autoscalers](#)  
> **Last Updated**: 2026-08-28

## Introduction

This page outlines the comparative analysis and migration strategies employed by Netflix regarding dual Apache Flink autoscalers. Operating over 30,000 jobs across multiple AWS regions, efficient autoscaling is essential to balance resource expenditure with performance requirements while minimizing manual intervention. Netflix currently manages both a legacy in-house autoscaler and the Apache Flink community autoscaler, with a strategic goal to converge on the open-source solution to reduce long-term maintenance burdens. Key use cases encompass Data Mesh-generated pipelines and custom stateful workloads supporting personalization, advertising, and live event processing. The documentation further details engineering lessons learned concerning metrics selection, cost optimization, and the trade-offs between adopting community standards versus maintaining proprietary infrastructure.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: The Provisioning Dilemma at Massive Scale
*   **Peak vs. Average Trade-off:** Provisioning resources for peak load results in significant waste, whereas provisioning for average load causes performance lag during demand surges.
*   **Job Volume & Diversity:** Netflix operates over 30,000 Flink jobs spanning custom pipelines (e.g., Ads, Personalization) to standard Data Mesh-generated jobs, making manual management impossible.
*   **Dynamic Load Factors:** Workloads fluctuate due to daily cycles, product launches, and regional failovers, necessitating a solution that adapts automatically without human intervention.

### Concept 2: The Operational Cost of Scaling Actions
*   **Scaling is Non-Trivial:** A scaling operation is not instantaneous; it defaults to a complex sequence involving creating a savepoint, gracefully stopping the job, and restarting it at a new size.
*   **Latency Impact:** For stateful jobs with large amounts of state, the resize process can take minutes, creating a constraint on how frequently scaling can occur.
*   **Reliability Requirements:** The autoscaler must provide resources exactly when needed without breaking the job or causing data loss during the restart mechanism.

### Concept 3: Strategic Migration from Proprietary to Open Source
*   **Parallel Operation Strategy:** Netflix initially ran two distinct autoscalers simultaneously: a legacy in-house system built when no mature options existed, and the newer Apache Flink community autoscaler.
*   **Capability Comparison:** The open-source autoscaler proved capable of scaling workloads the homegrown system was not designed for, highlighting gaps in the proprietary solution.
*   **Convergence Plan:** The migration strategy involves steadily converging onto the open-source option to avoid the long-term maintenance costs of custom infrastructure, leveraging community innovation while running both systems in production during the transition.
